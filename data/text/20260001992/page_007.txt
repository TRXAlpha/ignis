In Figure 7 the smoke aerosol detected at the FFD tracks the flame length with a minor delay.  This limited delay 
is likely due to the direct path from the SFU to the FFD. Even though they were on opposite ends of the Cygnus 
module, the bulk of the flow went directly to the FFD without the extensive mixing seen in geometries like the ISS 
[12]. Consequently, the detection scenario tested here is likely to be quicker than in many spacecraft. Figure 8 
compares the HRR from the fire  with the radiometer for the same 2-sided test in SAFFIRE VI. The peak HRR hit 3 
kW which is substantial for a small spacecraft.  However, as seen in Table 1, the temperature rise at the FFD was only 
0.5 °C. Larger temperatures (peak of 77 °C ) were seen at the outlet of the SFU [9] but they quickly reduced due to 
mixing and heat loss.
